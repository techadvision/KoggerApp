# Motor noise, depth loss and the "clockwork" artefacts - analysis of Andy's 3 Nov 2026 logs

Analysis only, no code changed. Sources: `testlogs/motor_noise_and_depth_loss/` (app log `pulse-andy_noise.log`, three 2D plogs of 3 Nov 11:28 / 11:42 / 11:56, two screenshots), Pulse Black on a serial link at 115200 baud, app 2.01, branch `feature/pulse-depth-source`. Code read: `src/id_binnary.cpp`, `src/black_stripes_processor.cpp`, `src/dataset.cpp` (gates, addChart), `src/depth_gates.h`, `src/data_processor/bottom_track_processor.cpp`, `src/epoch.h` (chartFrom/gainSource), `src/scene2d/echogram_tvg.*`, `echogram_watercolumn.*`, `echogram_sidescan_tvg.h`, `qml/PulseDepthEngine.qml`.

(7 Nov 2026: the testlogs folder was lost on 6 Nov and restored from the tablet; the sources now live in `testlogs/Andy_noise/`, `testlogs/plogs 061126/`, `testlogs/Problem check/` (the 18 Oct recordings) and `testlogs/app logs/`. The screenshots were not recoverable.)

## Summary

The motor noise is real, but it is not what produces the clockwork depth loss, nor the vertical artefacts. Three independent mechanisms were found, all verifiable in the logs and in the code, and they lock together into the pattern Andy filmed.

1. **Gate A is incompatible with the dynamic range planner beyond ~4 m of depth.** Gate A rejects a bottom deeper than 0.75 x 0.9 = 0.675 of the acquired range. The planner sizes the range to depth + 2 m (depth + 3 m right after deepening a metre step). D/(D+2) exceeds 0.675 for every D > 4.15 m; D/(D+3) for every D > 6.2 m. So past ~4 m the real bottom is rejected as "the search edge", the readout dashes after 2 s, the search doubles the range after 3 s, the bottom is found, the planner shrinks the range back to depth + 2, and the bottom is rejected again. That cycle is the clockwork, and every lap of it is a resolution change. This is the "lost depth after a certain depth". (Section 9: this is the depth + 2 branch, i.e. "second echo optimize" off, which is the persistent default; with it on the range is 2 x depth + 2 and the gate is never reached.)
2. **~40% of pings arrive damaged on this serial link** (81% nominal load). Each ping is three 200-sample frames; the first frame always arrives, the second and third are dropped often. A lost middle frame is zero-filled (a dark block at 1/3-2/3 of the range), a lost tail frame shortens the epoch (a dark block from 2/3 or 1/3 of the range downwards). The black-stripes repair normally hides this, but its reference is cleared on every resolution change, so the first damaged pings after each change render as the dark "long line down" - exactly at the resolution shift.
3. **The bottom track resets its 5-epoch window on every resolution change** and the first epochs after it cannot clear the (window-sized) threshold: one or more "no bottom" epochs follow every `DYNAMIC: plan`. On its own this is a transient; combined with (1) it keeps the readout at a dash longer. (Corrected 7 Nov, see section 3.)

The 2D TVG and the water-body filter are implemented soundly and are not the cause. They do make the picture worse under motor noise, for one specific reason: the 2D TVG has no noise-floor subtraction (the side scan TVG has one), so a flat electrical noise floor is amplified with depth, and the water-body filter stops at the bottom, so the band under the bottom becomes the brightest noise on screen. With the water-body filter on, the "filter" slider no longer drives the upstream low-level cut, so there is no way to subtract the floor while TVG is on. That is the overlooked ability.

## 1. Gate A against the planner - the clockwork

`depth_gates.h`: `gateA(bt) = bt > 0.75 x min(maxDistance, 0.9 x rangeM)`, with `rangeM = offset + samples x resolution` of the epoch's own trace (`dataset.cpp:1834`). On a red/black `maxDistance` is 50 m, so the ceiling is 0.675 x acquired range.

`PulseDepthEngine.qml`: `calculateDynamicResolution` wants `500 x round((depth + margin) x 2)` mm = depth + 2 m (margin default 2); `updateDynamicResolutionWithStep` adds +2 to `candidateRes` (= +1 m) when the metre level went deeper, and only re-plans when `floor(depth)` changes a level after `requiredStableReading` (3) readings.

The arithmetic: 0.675 (D + 2) > D only while D < 4.15 m; 0.675 (D + 3) > D only while D < 6.23 m. Beyond that the planned range puts the real bottom past the gate.

What the log shows, 11:43:14-11:43:21 (the 11:42 plog): bottom track 4.06-4.32 m raw of 6.00 m acquired (ratio 0.68-0.72), "gate A rejected: far end" for 6 s -> `DEPTH: no bottom for 2 s - dash` -> `DYNAMIC: search - no bottom for 3.1 s, range 6.0 -> 12.0 m` -> `search found a bottom at 4.36 m` 0.5 s later -> `plan shallow 6.5 m -> 6.6 m` (ceiling 4.455 m) -> stable only until the depth creeps past 4.45. The same lap repeats at 4.60 of 6.60, 5.07 of 7.20, 6.5-7.1 of 9.0, 7.5-7.7 of 9.6. The log holds 25 search laps, 333 gate-A lines, 56 dashes; the gate-A rejections cluster at ratios 0.68-0.80 (real bottoms) and 0.86-0.90 (true far-end noise, i.e. the gate doing its job).

The ceiling is visible statistically: over 1206 `DEPTH: every 10 s` lines the ratio shown depth / acquiring range piles up at 0.55-0.65 and falls off a cliff at 0.675 (33 lines at 0.65, 3 at 0.70). Nothing above the ceiling survives except held readouts during a search.

Why it never showed before: the 18 Oct recordings that set 0.75 / 0.9 were 3.5 m (gravel pit) and the shallows. The note in the gate ("real bottoms reached 0.64 of the range on the red, only right after the scheme shrank the range") was true there and is the general case past 4 m.

A feedback loop makes it worse: while the bottom is rejected the depth engine sees NaN, so the planner never learns the depth crossed the next metre level and never widens the range on its own - only the search does, 3 s later, by doubling.

Note also what the device's own depth (the rangefinder / DBT) did in the same seconds: 25.5, 43.4, 39.6, 41.5, 44.4 m over a 4.2 m bottom. The device's internal detector is lost in the motor noise too, so it is no fallback here (it is only a witness in the gates, correctly).

## 2. Frame loss on the serial link - the dark blocks and the "long line down"

The plogs (11:42 file, 6471 pings): every ping is 3 frames of 200 samples. 1142 pings (17.6%) lost the middle frame (zero-filled 200-400), ~1100 lost the tail frame (epoch of 400 samples), ~400 lost both (200 samples); the first frame was lost 13 times in total. 74-76% of the pings are complete in all three files. The app's own `LINK:` line agrees: "chart samples lost 12-19% (10 s)" at "81% of 115200", 13.9 pings/s. A first frame that always arrives and later frames that drop is the signature of a transmit buffer that cannot drain 600 samples in 72 ms plus framing.

How a damaged ping renders (`id_binnary.cpp` IDBinChart::parsePayload): a gap inside a ping is zero-filled and listed in `errList`; a lost tail is not zero-filled - the ping completes short (`m_chartSize = m_chartSizeIncr`), so the epoch's range is 4.0 or 2.0 m instead of 6.0. `chartFrom` draws zeros past `rawSize`. With the "F Dark"/"S Dark" palettes zero is the darkest blue: the rectangular blocks in the second screenshot, with edges at 1/3 and 2/3 of the range, and the dark region under the bottom with periodic full columns in the first.

Why they coincide with the resolution shift: `dataset.cpp:418-447` clears the black-stripes reference (`clearEthalonData`) forward and backward whenever the resolution or offset differs from the previous epoch. Right after a resolution change there is nothing to repair from, so the first damaged pings stay dark (and short), and only once 1-3 complete pings have rebuilt the reference does the repair resume. Mechanism (1) changes the resolution on every lap, so the dark column lands on the same frame as the dash.

The same short epochs also matter for gate A: a 400-sample epoch has `rangeM` 4.0 m, so any bottom past 2.7 m is "far end" for it. In the steady state the black-stripes grow path (`dataSize < newDataSize`) restores 600 samples before the gates run, which is why gate A does not fire on 25% of the epochs; after a resolution change it does.

## 3. The bottom track's window reset

`bottom_track_processor.cpp:278-302`: on a resolution change `summ`, `cash` and `epoch_counter` are cleared. The threshold stays `threshold_int x windowSize` while the sum holds only 1..4 epochs, so the first epochs after each change rarely clear it: the log has a `BT: epoch N | bottom track - m raw of X m acquired (-) | no bottom` 0.3 s after essentially every `DYNAMIC: plan`. A transient on its own (the readout holds 2 s), but it adds to the dash in every lap of (1).

Short epochs inside the window are handled tolerably (the sum simply lacks that epoch past its end); mixed resolutions are not an issue because of the reset.

**Corrected 7 Nov 2026 (P4 of the plan).** The threshold is not the mechanism: both profiles carry `threshold = 0` (`distProcPulseRed[6]`, `distProcPulseBlue[6]`), so `threshold_int` is 0 and the search accepts any positive sum. The bench (`bt.py`, the port) shows where the "no bottom" epochs really are: the last W/2 = 2 epochs of the OLD resolution before each change (Andy's 11:42 plog: 70 of its 72 NaN epochs sit at offsets -2/-1 from the 36 changes, none at the first new epoch; Olav's 16:06: 236 of 238 at -2/-1 from 118 changes). The window is centred - epoch j is published when epoch j + W/2 has been summed - and the reset clears the counter, so those two epochs never reach their publication point in any pass. It is the partial-window gap the tracker has at its start, mirrored at the end. The remedy is to drain the old window at the reset (search it for the pending W/2 epochs as the partial window does at the start); on the bench that takes both counts to zero without changing any epoch that already had a value.

## 4. The 2D TVG and the water-body filter

**Implementation.** `EchogramTvg::apply` is a stateless per-epoch gain 10^(k z / 20), z = offset + i x resolution, capped at 30x, cached per epoch with a size + version guard; the cache is invalidated by the black-stripes repair (`invalidateDerived`). Dynamic resolution is handled correctly because the gain is a function of metres, not of sample index, and each epoch carries its own resolution. The water-body filter (`EchogramWaterColumn::apply`) runs on the gain-shaped buffer, above `bottomProcessing.getDistance()` only, with a thread-local fallback to the last bottom for epochs stored as NaN. Neither module depends on the range plan, the window, or the frame assembly. No defect was found in either that could produce stripes, blocks or the depth flapping, and nothing in them changes the depth.

**Where the combination hurts under motor noise.** The TVG law is a propagation-loss compensation; it assumes what it amplifies is echo. Motor EMI is an additive, range-flat floor at the receiver, so TVG turns it into fog that brightens with depth (1.9x at 6 m, 2.6x at 9 m, 3.5x at 12 m at the default 0.9 dB/m). The water-body filter dims that fog above the bottom (and the bottom guard keeps the shoulder), but it copies everything below the bottom verbatim by design (second echo, hardness). The result is a band under the bottom that is brighter than the water column - the "very systematic" artefact under the bottom in both screenshots. It is systematic because it is deterministic: same depth, same gain, same floor.

**The overlooked ability.** `EchogramSideScanTvg` has a per-ping noise-floor subtraction before the gain (median of a water-column window, scaled by a strength) precisely because "TVG amplifies the far-field noise". `EchogramTvg` has nothing equivalent. And `PulseRuntimeSettings.echogramWaterBodyFilterEnabled` pins the upstream low-level cut to 0 while the water-body filter drives the "filter" slider, so with the filter on there is no floor subtraction anywhere in the 2D chain. The configuration Olav proposes for noisy users (TVG off, water-body filter off, a high filter) works because it is the only path where the slider still subtracts a floor. A floor subtraction in the 2D TVG path (as on side scan) would give the same relief without giving up TVG, the water-body filter, second echo and hardness.

A second, smaller interaction: at every resolution change the renderer's downsampling window changes (10 vs 12 vs 20 mm per sample), so the averaged noise texture and brightness step at the switch. Visible as a seam, not as a stripe.

The first screenshot shows 0.4 m over a 5-6 m bottom. That is not a gate-A value (those are NaN, i.e. a dash). The candidates are the rangefinder taking over as chosen source with a reading in its own ringdown zone, or the tracker's floor; the 11:47 shore approach in the log shows `SHALLOW` lines with the rangefinder at 1.1-1.5 m while the bottom track read 1.5-2.2 m. Without the log of that video it cannot be settled; worth one eye on `DEPTH: chosen source` lines when the field test reproduces it.

## 6. What the field test can confirm, without any code change

Depth is the discriminator. In water deeper than 4.5 m with the motor off and second echo optimize off, if the readout dashes every ~4 s and the `BT:` lines say "gate A rejected" at ratios 0.68-0.80 of the acquired range while `DYNAMIC: search` laps, mechanism (1) is confirmed independent of the motor. The `LINK:` line's "chart samples lost" shows mechanism (2) independent of depth; a lower chart sample count or a longer period (load under ~60%) should make the dark blocks disappear. Toggling TVG and the water-body filter should change the brightness of the band under the bottom and nothing about the dash cadence.

Likely remedies, for the discussion after the test (none applied): plan the range with the gate in mind (range >= depth / 0.675 plus margin, or gate A at a fraction of the planned range rather than 0.675 of the acquired one, or a gate-A fraction that grows with depth), keep the black-stripes reference across a resolution change by rescaling it instead of clearing it, scale the bottom-track threshold by the epochs actually in the window after a reset, add a noise-floor subtraction to the 2D TVG path, and a link plan that keeps serial load under the point where tail frames drop.

## 7. Addendum, 6 Nov - the replay on Olav's phone and the link question

Dash, column and resolution shift are the same lap of the same cycle, which is why they always coincide. The second picture has the same column at the left, and the two rangefinder spikes to 44 m are the device's own "nothing found".

**The frame loss is Andy's link, not the motor and not the device.** Over the 11:42 plog, 43% of pings are damaged in nearly every 10-second window, quiet stretches (water-column mean 7) and noisy ones (87) alike; the correlation between loss and noise is 0.32 and the Fletcher checksum fails on zero frames out of 45 002. ## 8. The gate-A fix space (analysis, for the plan)

The false bottoms gate A exists for sit at 0.78-0.90 of the range (the shore, 18 Oct) and real bottoms at depth + 2 m sit at 0.67-0.80 from 4 m to 8 m. The two overlap, so no single fraction of the acquired range separates them; raising 0.75 alone trades the shore back in. Three ways that do separate them, to choose between in the plan:

(a) Make the planner respect the gate: range >= depth / 0.675 + margin, e.g. margin = max(2 m, 0.6 x depth), so the bottom never sits above ~0.62 of the range by construction. Cheapest, no gate change; costs resolution in deep water (an 8 m bottom gets a 13 m range).

(b) Judge gate A against the planned range rather than the acquired one, i.e. the reachable end is the range the scheme asked for; then a short or truncated epoch cannot reject a bottom the plan covers. Fixes the truncated-epoch case too, but still leaves depth + 2 at 0.78 for 7 m, so it needs (a) as well.

(c) Let the search result stick: after `search found a bottom`, re-plan from the found depth with the deepening rule (+3) and hold it for a stable period instead of returning to depth + 2 at once. Breaks the lap but not the ceiling at 6.2 m.

(a) is the one that removes the ceiling; (b) and (c) are belts. Any of them also stops the search laps, and with them the resolution changes that expose the black-stripes reset and the bottom-track window reset.

## 9. Why Olav's 2-6 m logs never hit the gate: "second echo optimize"

Olav's own BT lines from a 2-6 m section (6 Nov) read ratio max 0.41-0.47 at 5.4-6.7 m, i.e. an acquired range of 13-15 m for a 6 m bottom. That is not depth + 2; it is 2 x depth + 2, which is what `calculateDynamicResolution` plans when `pulseSettings.doubleEchoOptimize` is on (`PulseDepthEngine.qml:293`). With the doubling the bottom sits at D / (2D + 2) < 0.5 of the range at every depth, so gate A (0.675) can never reach it. Without it the range is D + 2 and the ceiling bites past 4.15 m.

Andy's log has the switch: `SETTINGS: persistent doubleEchoOptimize -> false` at 11:40:17 on 3 Nov, two and a half minutes before the 11:42 plog (he had toggled it on and off on 25 and 30 Oct as well). Every `range wanted` line in his sessions is D + 2. So the two boats are a clean A/B of one setting: second echo optimize on (Olav) - no gate-A rejections at 6.7 m; off (Andy) - the clockwork from 4.15 m. The persistent default is off (`PulseSettings.qml:130`), so a user who never touches the row gets Andy's behaviour. The fix therefore cannot be "leave it on": the planner's D + 2 branch must itself keep the bottom under the gate (option (a) in section 8), independent of the second-echo preference, which is a display/sampling choice and not a correctness one.

## 10. Olav's 6 Nov field test - both "must fix" effects are the gate-A stretches

Five plogs (red external 15:42 and 15:46, black external 15:51 and 15:55, built-in red 16:06), app log `pulse06112026.log`, 3-9 m, second echo optimize off except where noted. The counts per settings stretch, from the app log:

With TVG and the water-body filter on (15:46:06-15:47:47, red): 10 search laps, 11 dashes, 43 gate-A lines in 100 s. With both off (15:47:47-15:51:18): 8 laps, 9 dashes, 37 gate-A lines in 210 s - the laps continue unchanged when the display chain is off. Black external 15:55-15:59 (almost no noise): 20 laps, 21 dashes, 82 gate-A lines. Built-in red 16:06-16:10 (no noise): 25 laps, 27 dashes, 115 gate-A lines. Then second echo optimize on at 16:10:18: zero gate-A lines, zero laps, zero dashes for the rest of the outing (ratio max 0.63, then 0.48). The 15:51 black log that "found no bottom" is the device swap, not a data problem.

So the lap reproduces on a clean device with no motor noise, stops the moment the range doubles, and is indifferent to TVG and the filter. What the two display modules do is make the lap's consequences visible or not:

**The deep "bottom" columns are the second echo in the search-burst epochs.** The screenshot of the 16:06 replay shows it directly: the long columns reach 16-17 m and carry a red contour at exactly twice the bottom's depth. Those epochs were acquired at the doubled search range, which is where the second echo lands, and the TVG lifts it to the bottom's own brightness (gain 2.3-2.6x at 14-17 m). With TVG off the same epochs are still there (8 laps in the TVG-off stretch) but the second echo is too faint to see. Olav's reading "correctly rendered bottom area, much deeper than it should be" is exactly right - it is a correctly rendered second echo. The fix is not in the TVG; it is that the search burst should not happen over a found bottom (P1), and a floor subtraction (P5) is a separate matter.

**The stairs along the contour are the water-body filter's stale bottom.** `EchogramWaterColumn::apply` filters above the epoch's stored bottom (`bottomProcessing.getDistance()`), and for an epoch stored as NaN it falls back to the last finite bottom seen on the render thread (`t_lastBottomM`, meant for black-stripe-patched epochs). Gate A stores whole stretches as NaN, so every epoch in a stretch is filtered against the last accepted bottom before it. On a downward slope that reference is shallower than the real bottom, so the zone between them is left undimmed - the bright strip on top of the contour, growing as the slope deepens through the stretch; on an upward slope the reference is deeper, so the dim reaches below the real contour - the dark strip under it. Each search lap accepts one bottom and starts a new step, which is why they are stairs and why they coincide with the deep columns. Turning the filter off removes the strips because nothing is being dimmed; the gate-A stretches are still there. The stairs are a second reason, after the readout, that the gate must not blank a bottom the planner caused to be rejected - and a reason the display filter should take a reference that is never blanked (the raw bottom track, or the last value interpolated across a NaN stretch with a short hold like the readout's), added to the plan as P1b.
